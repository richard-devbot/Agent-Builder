# RPG / IBM i Modernization Playbook

A 10-phase playbook for modernizing RPG applications off IBM i (AS/400, iSeries, Power systems). Like COBOL/mainframe modernization, this requires specialized knowledge — but with important differences. RPG IV is more modern than most COBOL, DB2 for i is integrated and more standard, and **5250 green-screen UIs are a significant migration concern unique to this platform**.

This playbook walks through **strategy selection first** (the four canonical strategies adapted for IBM i), then deep-dives the **rewrite path** that most modernization projects take.

## When to use this playbook

- You have RPG applications on IBM i (Power systems, formerly AS/400 / iSeries)
- Drivers include hardware EOL, workforce risk, agility, customer demand for modern UI, or cloud strategy
- You're considering modernization but haven't committed to an approach
- The project is multi-year (typical) — anything &lt;12 months should question scope

## When NOT to use this playbook

- IBM i is operationally fine and not under threat — modernization for its own sake rarely pays off
- The RPG codebase is small (&lt;50K LOC, &lt;30 programs) — handle as a custom-app migration
- You've already committed to a specific path (replatform via PowerVS, etc.) — see targeted resources for that approach

## RPG vs COBOL: what's similar, what's different

If you're applying lessons from [COBOL modernization](/playbook/cobol-modernization-playbook), expect overlap and differences:

**Similar:** multi-year timelines (4-8 quarters minimum), strategy selection matters more than execution, business rule extraction is the highest-value upfront work, workforce transition is its own phase, specialist expertise required.

**Different:**
- **RPG IV is more modern.** RPG free-format reads similarly to other languages; cleaner translation than older COBOL.
- **DB2 for i is integrated and more standard.** Easier to migrate than VSAM/IMS.
- **5250 green screens are huge.** Subfile migration to web is its own discipline; UI work is significant.
- **PowerVS is a real cloud option.** IBM i in IBM Cloud / Azure (via Skytap) is feasible (replatform without leaving IBM i).
- **Replace strategy is more common.** SAP, JD Edwards, Infor, Workday all replace RPG-based ERP regularly.
- **Vendor ecosystem is different.** IBM Power systems sales org is separate from IBM Z.

## The four strategies (decide first)

Before writing a line of code, determine which strategy fits. The 10 phases that follow assume you've chosen **rewrite** (the most common modernization path).

### Strategy 1 — Replatform (lift-and-shift)

Move IBM i workloads off owned hardware to **IBM PowerVS** (IBM i in IBM Cloud), **Skytap** (IBM i in Azure), or managed IBM i hosting. The RPG stays. The on-prem hardware goes.

**Right when:** cost reduction is primary, code is well-understood and stable, no significant agility needs, low risk appetite, 12-24 month timeline available. **Wrong when:** you want to rewrite eventually, the application needs significant changes, vendor lock-in to PowerVS is unacceptable. Typical: $3-10M one-time, 30-50% cost savings, 2-4 year payback.

### Strategy 2 — Refactor (automated translation)

Use tooling (Fresche / X-Migrate, ARCAD Transformer, AveriSource, AWS Blu Age) to translate RPG / CL / DDS to Java or C#. Output is functional but not idiomatic.

**Right when:** need off IBM i AND off RPG, can't spend years in rewrite, accept "ugly modern code" you'll improve. **Wrong when:** code quality matters from day 1, application needs restructuring, team can't accept tool-generated code. Typical: $400K-$1.5M tooling, $4-12M services.

### Strategy 3 — Rewrite (reverse-engineer + re-implement)

Understand rules, design from scratch, implement modern. Use RPG as the behavior reference. **The rest of this playbook focuses here.**

**Right when:** architecture itself is part of the problem, want cloud-native patterns, have leadership support and multi-year appetite, sufficient SME availability. **Wrong when:** insufficient business-rule knowledge, tight timeline, no SME access, high regulatory burden requiring parity proof. Typical: $12-40M+, 3-6 years.

### Strategy 4 — Replace (commercial off-the-shelf)

Buy an ERP / vertical-industry product that does what your RPG does. Migrate data in. Retire RPG.

**Right when:** mature COTS exists in your industry (distribution → SAP / Infor M3, manufacturing → JDE / SAP, banking → core banking COTS), customizations aren't truly differentiated. **Wrong when:** heavy customizations are differentiating, COTS doesn't fit the model. Typical: $400K-$8M/year license, $5-25M implementation.

### Decision matrix

| Driver | Replatform | Refactor | Rewrite | Replace |
|--------|-----------|----------|---------|---------|
| Cost reduction primary | ✅ Best | ⚠️ Maybe | ❌ Wrong tool | ⚠️ Maybe |
| Workforce risk (no RPG skills) | ⚠️ Defers | ✅ Good | ✅ Good | ✅ Good |
| Need agility / new features | ❌ No help | ⚠️ Some | ✅ Best | ✅ If COTS fits |
| Tight timeline (&lt;2 years) | ✅ Good | ✅ Good | ❌ Won't make it | ⚠️ Depends |
| Behavior parity required (regulatory) | ✅ Best | ✅ Good | ❌ Hard | ⚠️ Depends |
| Custom logic is differentiator | ⚠️ N/A | ⚠️ N/A | ✅ Preserves | ❌ Loses |
| 5250 → web is mandatory | ❌ No help | ⚠️ Some help | ✅ Full redesign | ✅ COTS UI |
| Strong leadership / governance | Any | Any | ✅ Required | ✅ Required |

For your specific situation, document why one strategy fits and others don't.

## Critical success factors (rewrite path)

1. **Strong leadership sponsorship** — multi-year projects need political air cover.
2. **Clear strategy decision upfront** — don't drift between strategies; pick one and commit.
3. **Adequate budget** — underfunded RPG modernizations fail predictably.
4. **Senior engineers who can read RPG** — the scarcest resource. Hire / retain / train.
5. **Business stakeholder availability** — rule extraction needs SME time; without it the project stalls.
6. **5250 UI redesign treated as first-class work** — not an afterthought.
7. **Realistic timeline expectations** — set them at the start, not when you're 6 months over.
8. **Automation tooling** — manual analysis of 1M+ lines is impossibly slow. Invest in tools.

## When to engage specialists

Even with this playbook, RPG modernization usually requires specialist expertise. Engage when:

- **Initial strategy decision** — independent perspective on which fits.
- **Tooling selection** — replatform vendors, refactoring tools, CDC tools all have nuances.
- **Architectural decisions** — especially the 5250 UI strategy.
- **Workforce transition** — HR and change management for this kind of shift is its own discipline.
- **Compliance / audit** — regulators evaluating modernization need specialized framing.

This playbook gives you the structure; specialist consultants give you the expertise. They're complementary.
