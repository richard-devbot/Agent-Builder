# COBOL Modernization Playbook

A 10-phase playbook for modernizing a COBOL application off the mainframe. Unlike .NET migrations (where the team usually knows the legacy stack), COBOL modernizations require specialized knowledge, careful strategy selection, and longer timelines.

This playbook walks through **strategy selection first** (the four canonical strategies below), then deep-dives the **rewrite path** that most modernization projects take.

## When to use this playbook

- You have COBOL applications running on mainframe (z/OS, AS/400, Tandem)
- Drivers include cost reduction, workforce risk, agility, or compliance
- You're considering modernization but haven't committed to an approach
- The project is multi-year (typical) — anything &lt;12 months should question scope

## When NOT to use this playbook

- Mainframe is operationally fine and not under threat — modernization for its own sake rarely pays off
- The COBOL is small (&lt;50K LOC, &lt;50 programs) — may be simpler to handle as a custom-app migration
- You've already committed to a specific path (replatform, etc.) — see the targeted resources for that approach

## The four strategies (decide first)

Before writing a line of code, determine which of the four canonical strategies fits. This decision drives everything else; the 10 phases that follow assume you've chosen **rewrite** (the most common modernization path).

### Strategy 1 — Replatform (lift-and-shift)

Move COBOL execution from mainframe to commodity hardware (x86 Linux in cloud) using a runtime like Micro Focus Enterprise Server, IBM Wazi, AWS Mainframe Modernization, Heirloom, or Asysco AMT. The COBOL stays. The mainframe goes.

**Right when:** cost reduction is primary, code is well-understood and stable, no significant agility needs, low risk appetite. **Wrong when:** you want to rewrite eventually, the application needs significant changes, or vendor lock-in to the replatform tooling is unacceptable. Typical: $5-15M one-time, 30-60% MIPS savings, 2-4 year payback.

### Strategy 2 — Refactor (automated translation)

Use tools (AWS Blu Age, Asysco AMT, Modern Systems / Astadia) to automatically translate COBOL to Java or C#. Output is functional but not idiomatic.

**Right when:** need to get off mainframe AND off COBOL, can't spend years in rewrite, accept "ugly modern code" you'll improve. **Wrong when:** code quality matters from day 1, application needs restructuring, team can't accept tool-generated code in production. Typical: $500K-$2M tooling, $5-15M services.

### Strategy 3 — Rewrite (reverse-engineer + re-implement)

Understand the rules, design from scratch, implement modern. Use COBOL as the behavior reference. **The rest of this playbook focuses here.**

**Right when:** architecture itself is part of the problem, want cloud-native patterns, have leadership support and multi-year appetite, sufficient SME availability. **Wrong when:** insufficient business-rule knowledge, tight timeline, no SME access, regulatory burden requiring parity proof (rewrite makes parity HARDER). Typical: $15-50M+, 3-7 years, industry failure rate 50-70%.

### Strategy 4 — Replace (commercial off-the-shelf)

Buy software that does what your COBOL does (Guidewire, Temenos, SAP, Epic). Migrate data in. Retire COBOL.

**Right when:** mature COTS exists in your industry, customizations aren't truly differentiated, willingness to change processes to fit the COTS. **Wrong when:** heavy customizations are competitively differentiated, COTS doesn't fit the model, customizing recreates the maintenance burden. Typical: $500K-$10M/year license, $5-30M implementation.

### Decision matrix

| Driver | Replatform | Refactor | Rewrite | Replace |
|--------|-----------|----------|---------|---------|
| Cost reduction primary | ✅ Best | ⚠️ Maybe | ❌ Wrong tool | ⚠️ Maybe |
| Workforce risk (no COBOL skills) | ⚠️ Defers | ✅ Good | ✅ Good | ✅ Good |
| Need agility / new features | ❌ No help | ⚠️ Some | ✅ Best | ✅ If COTS fits |
| Tight timeline (&lt;2 years) | ✅ Good | ✅ Good | ❌ Won't make it | ⚠️ Depends |
| Behavior parity required (regulatory) | ✅ Best | ✅ Good | ❌ Hard | ⚠️ Depends |
| Custom logic is differentiator | ⚠️ N/A | ⚠️ N/A | ✅ Preserves | ❌ Loses |
| Strong leadership / governance | Any | Any | ✅ Required | ✅ Required |

For your specific situation, document why one strategy fits and others don't.

## Critical success factors (rewrite path)

1. **Strong leadership sponsorship** — multi-year projects need political air cover.
2. **Clear strategy decision upfront** — don't drift between strategies; pick one and commit.
3. **Adequate budget** — underfunded COBOL modernizations fail predictably.
4. **Senior engineers who can read COBOL** — the scarcest resource. Hire / retain / train.
5. **Business stakeholder availability** — rule extraction needs SME time; without it the project stalls.
6. **Realistic timeline expectations** — set them at the start, not when you're 6 months over.
7. **Automation tooling** — manual analysis of millions of lines is impossibly slow. Invest in tools.
8. **Risk-aware culture** — mainframe ops culture is risk-averse for good reasons. Bring some of that to the modern stack rather than discarding it.

## When to engage specialists

Even with this playbook, COBOL modernization usually requires specialist expertise. Engage when:

- **Initial strategy decision** — independent perspective on which fits.
- **Tooling selection** — replatform vendors, refactoring tools, CDC tools all have nuances.
- **Architectural decisions** — cloud-native architecture for regulated industries has specific patterns.
- **Workforce transition** — HR and change management for this kind of shift is its own discipline.
- **Compliance / audit** — regulators evaluating modernization need specialized framing.

This playbook gives you the structure; specialist consultants give you the expertise. They're complementary.
